What is Matchbook?

A deliberate-practice system for League of Legends — not a recorder.

Matchbook isn't a stat tracker, a recorder, or a coach. It's the layer that turns your games into actual learning. Recording is one feature inside it — the system is what you do with the footage: flag it, review it, write down what you'd do differently, and carry that into the next game.

🎬

Record

Games record automatically when they start. No record button, no setup, nothing to remember.

📍

Flag

One hotkey mid-game drops a timestamped flag on the moment. Keep playing.

🔍

Review

Jump straight to your flagged moments after the game — no scrubbing through 30 minutes of footage.

📖

Journal

Structured reflection, with your match stats pulled in automatically from Riot's API.

Stop playing the same game over and over.

You know the pattern. Queue, lose a game you've lost a hundred times before, mutter something about your jungler, queue again.

Another stats dashboard won't break it — you've probably tried those, plus the guides, maybe coaching. The problem was never knowledge. It's that nothing connects what you know to what you do in game. That connection is the whole product.

You can't fix what you won't see.

Mid-game, when something goes sideways — a fight you shouldn't have taken, a wave you misread — press ⌘J. Matchbook drops a timestamped flag into the recording. Don't stop playing. Don't try to remember it.

After the game, the moment is sitting there waiting for you. Watching yourself make the mistake is uncomfortable. It's also the fastest way to stop making it.

Make every game actually count.

Before you queue, set a focus — one thing you're working on for this block of games. Every game imports automatically with the stats attached: KDA, CS, vision, gold. Pulled from Riot's API, no manual entry.

Afterward, a structured journal: what you saw, what you were thinking, what you'd do differently. Two minutes of honest review beats another autopilot queue.

Close the loop on your learning.

Notice it in game. Flag it. Review it. Write down what you'd do differently. Carry that into the next game — and check, over weeks, whether it's actually changing.

Matchbook tracks your learning objectives, champion notes, and progress over time, so improvement stops being a feeling and starts being something you can see.

Already record with OBS? Keep it.

Matchbook works alongside whatever recorder you already use — it's a deliberate-practice system, not just a recorder. Your streaming or clipping setup stays exactly as it is. Matchbook handles the practice layer: the flags, the review, the journal. Run both.

Coaches: make your hour stick.

A good coaching session changes a player's mind in sixty minutes. Then twenty hours of solo queue undo it.

Matchbook lives in those twenty hours. Your students flag their own moments, journal between sessions, and show up to the next call with specific timestamps instead of "idk, I just lost."
